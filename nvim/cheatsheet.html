nvim cheatsheet

David’s setup · leader is Space

The one concept: nvim has modes. In Normal mode, letters are commands, not text. That is the entire learning curve.

Esc always returns to Normal — press it whenever you are lost. Press Space alone and wait: a menu of every custom command pops up.

Modes

EscNormal — moving & commands. You start here — your panic button
iInsert — actually typing text
vVisual — selecting text
:Command — save, quit, replace

Folding — collapse sections

zaToggle the fold under the cursor★ the only one you really need
zo / zcOpen / close this fold
z⇧R / z⇧MOpen all / close all folds★ capitals — needs Shift
zr / zmOpen / close one level at a time
z⇧AToggle this fold and everything nested inside it
zj / zkJump to the next / previous fold
zxReset every fold back to the default level★ undoes your manual opening/closing
zvOpen just enough to reveal the cursor
zfCreate a fold — from a v selection, or with a motion (zf5j = next 5 lines)
zd / zEDelete this fold / all folds (manual folds only)

Capitals go all the way, lowercase goes one step. z⇧R opens every fold, zr opens one level; z⇧M closes every fold, zm closes one. Same rule for z⇧A vs za.
Your working set: za while reading, z⇧R to see the whole file, zx to snap back to the outline.
In markdown, folds follow your headings automatically and open to foldlevel = 1 — H1 sections expanded, H2 and deeper collapsed, like an outline. Change the number in lua/options.lua: 0 shows only H1 lines, 2 goes down to H3, 99 opens everything. Everywhere else folding is manual: nothing to open until you make a fold with zf.
